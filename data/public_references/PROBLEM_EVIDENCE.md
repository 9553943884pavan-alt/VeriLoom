# Public Problem Evidence

The canonical machine-readable records are in `problem_evidence.jsonl`. All four records are marked `ingestible: false`: they support the hackathon problem analysis and must not be used as Nook & Kettle brand rules or facts.

The [2025 Content Marketing Institute enterprise survey](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/enterprise-research/enterprise-content-marketing-research-findings-2025) reports that 47% of surveyed enterprise marketers cited workflow or content-approval issues, and 61% cited communication across organisational silos as situational challenges. It also reports that 75% said their organisation used generative AI and 62% said their organisation had generative-AI usage guidelines. The article says its enterprise results cover 310 respondents, mainly from North America, drawn from 1,186 global responses fielded between 25 June and 16 August 2024. These numbers are self-reports from one survey; they are not four independent studies and do not establish causation or product effectiveness.

The [GOV.UK content-principles background](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background/govuk-content-principles-conventions-and-research-background) explains the operational reason for shared writing rules when many people create content. This is qualitative public-sector guidance, not a numerical estimate of brand damage or a substitute for interviews with intended users.

The pack contains concise original summaries and links. It does not reproduce the complete reports, download brand assets, or invent interview results. Use these references as initial supporting evidence and document any later first-hand research separately.
