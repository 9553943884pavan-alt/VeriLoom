# User Interview Guide

## Purpose

Use this guide to learn how content creators and reviewers currently prepare, check, revise, and approve organisation content. It is a research instrument, not a record of completed interviews. Do not fill in answers on behalf of participants.

## Opening and consent

“Thank you for speaking with me. I’m researching how teams review content for brand consistency and accuracy. Participation is voluntary. Please don’t share confidential company information, unpublished campaigns, customer information, or documents you are not authorised to share. I’ll record anonymised notes for this hackathon project. You can skip any question or stop at any time. May I take notes? May I record this conversation?”

If consent is not given, do not record. Take no notes beyond information the participant explicitly agrees may be noted.

## Questions for creators

Ask about actual past behaviour, not only what the participant thinks a tool should do.

1. What kinds of content do you create, and how often?
2. Who else contributes to or edits that content?
3. Please walk me through the most recent draft you prepared, from first draft to publication. Avoid showing confidential content.
4. How do you find the current brand rules, approved facts, terminology, and assets you need?
5. How do you check that claims and product details are correct?
6. What feedback or corrections do you commonly receive? Can you describe a recent example without sharing sensitive details?
7. What happens when guidance is missing, contradictory, or out of date?
8. How do you decide whether to accept a suggested rewrite? What would make a suggestion unhelpful?
9. How do you receive review comments and know what to change?
10. What is difficult about revision history or knowing whether content is approved?
11. Which formats do you work with most: text, images, documents, or other?
12. What would make an automated review useful or frustrating in your workflow?

## Questions for reviewers and managers

1. What content are you responsible for reviewing, and how frequently?
2. What do you check before approving content?
3. Where do the rules and approved facts you rely on come from? How do you know they are current?
4. Which issues are easiest to miss, and which issues take the most review time?
5. How do you explain a problem and request a correction today?
6. What evidence would you need to trust an automated flag?
7. When should a reviewer see a source citation, uncertainty notice, or request for clarification?
8. Which decisions must stay with a human reviewer?
9. How do you track revisions, approvals, and recurring problems?
10. What access restrictions should apply to drafts, guidelines, and review comments?
11. What would make a brand-review tool fit or fail your real workflow?

## Closing questions

- What important part of the workflow have I not asked about?
- May I contact you with a brief follow-up question? If yes, collect contact details separately from anonymised notes.
- Is there non-confidential public guidance you recommend I read?

## Research integrity

Do not claim an interview occurred until it has occurred. Do not turn assumptions into findings. In a presentation, report the number and type of participants, the method, dates, and limits of the sample. Paraphrase unless a participant has agreed to a quote.
